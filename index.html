Discover the Mount Arayat

Mount Arayat is an isolated, potentially active stratovolcano in the Central Luzon plains. Located within the vast agricultural lands of Pampanga, it rises to a height of 1,026 meters (3,366 ft) above sea level. Its southern half lies within the municipality of Arayat, while its northern half and summit are in Magalang. 10 km (6.21 mi) to the west of Mount Arayat is Angeles City and the former Clark Air Base. The active volcano Mount Pinatubo is located 26 km (16 mi) west, while Manila is located 75 km (47 mi) to the south. Mount Arayat was officially declared a national park in 1933 and a tourist spot in 1997. The mountain is currently under an immense deforestation threat.

Bale Batu

Bale Batu

Often called the “Little Cambodia of the Philippines,” Bale Batu is a stone structure at the foot of Mt. Arayat that looks more like a temple you’d find in Indonesia or Angkor Wat than anything Filipino. It’s one of the most unusual things you’ll see in Pampanga. ₱50 gets you a tour of the property, and it’s genuinely the kind of place you’ll want photos of because your friends won’t believe it’s in Arayat.

Sunflower Farm Arayat

For ₱25 entrance, you get to walk through fields of sunflowers in full bloom and honestly, that’s one of the best photo opportunities you’ll find in Pampanga at that price. It’s a working farm, not just a photo spot, so you can actually learn a bit about how sunflowers are grown and harvested while you’re there. Best visited early morning when the flowers are facing the sun and the light is soft.

Geological Information

Overview

Located between the municipalities of Arayat and Magalang in Pampanga, Mount Arayat stands alone in the middle of the Central Luzon plains. There are no recorded historical eruptions, and the volcano is classified as extinct, with its last volcanic activity dating back to the Holocene epoch.

Its collapsed caldera on the western side forms a dramatic horseshoe ridge featuring two primary summits: the North Peak (the highest point at 1,033 meters) and the South Peak, linked by a rugged knife-edge traverse.
